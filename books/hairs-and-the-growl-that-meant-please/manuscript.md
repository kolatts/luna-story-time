# Hairs and the Growl That Meant Please

*Dreamed up by Luna from Ohio*

A Dreamed Up By You standalone. Ages 4–8. Sixteen spreads.

**Refrain:** But scared is not the same as sure.

---

## 1. The rule about the Bramblewood

Every village has one rule it never explains.
Ours was this: *do not go past the thorn gate.*

Past the thorn gate was the Bramblewood,
and in the Bramblewood was the wolf,
and about the wolf there was only one word,
and everyone in the village used it.

*Hungry.*

---

## 2. What they said about Hairs

They said his coat was grey as an old rain.
They said his tail was black as a shut door.
They said his hair had been growing a hundred years
and had never once been brushed.

They said his name was Hairs.

Nobody in the village had ever met him.
That did not slow anybody down.

---

## 3. Princess Fern and Baby Bee

Princess Fern was seven, and a princess of the small kind:
one tin crown, slightly crooked, and a great deal of opinion.

Baby Bee was two. Baby Bee had one word.
She spent it carefully, the way you spend a last coin,
and some days she didn't spend it at all.

Thursdays were blackberry days.

---

## 4. Over the gate

The wind took Bee's grey blanket
and folded it neatly over the thorn gate,
the way wind does when it has plans.

Fern looked at the gate.
Fern looked at Bee.
Bee looked at the blanket.

"Fine," said Fern, to nobody. "Fine."

---

## 5. The trap

Inside, the wood was strung with ropes.
Not ropes — *hair.* Long silver hair, hooked on every thorn,
looped through every branch, drifted over every root
like a hundred years of somebody's morning.

Fern's boot caught. Then her cloak. Then her crown.

Something in the dark went **GRRRR.**

Fern's whole body said *run.*

> *But scared is not the same as sure.*

---

## 6. The growl

He came out slow, and he was enormous.
Grey as an old rain. Tail black as a shut door.
Hair pouring off him in long silver rivers
that went on into the trees and did not stop.

He opened his mouth.

**GRRRR,** said Hairs.

Fern shut her eyes and waited to be a story
that the village would tell.

---

## 7. What was actually happening

Nothing happened.

Nothing kept on happening
until Fern got curious enough to peek.

Hairs had not come one step closer.
He couldn't. Every thorn in the Bramblewood
held a piece of his hair, and he stood in the middle of it
like a kite in a tree.

The wolf was more caught than they were.

---

## 8. Hungry

"I'm hungry," said Hairs.

At least, that is what he meant.
What came out was **GRRRR,**
because a voice that hasn't been used in a hundred years
comes out the shape it was left in.

Fern took one step back. Her heart said *danger.*
Her eyes said *he's crying.*

> *But scared is not the same as sure.*

---

## 9. Bee spends her word

Bee walked out from behind her sister.
Two years old, bare feet, blanket under one arm.
She stood in front of the biggest wolf in the world
and looked all the way up.

Then Baby Bee spent her one word.

"Please," said Bee.

The whole wood went quiet to hear it.

---

## 10. The growl that meant please

And Hairs sat down. Just — sat down, in all that hair.

"That's the one," he said, and this time
Fern heard the words inside the growl.
"That's the word I've been practicing.
I've been practicing since before you were born, small one.
It keeps coming out wrong."

"Practicing to say what?" said Fern.

"*Please come to supper.* I've never had anyone."

---

## 11. Hungry for what

A hundred years of Thursdays.
A pot on, a fire lit, three bowls set out.
A hundred years of walking to the thorn gate
to ask, and hearing his own voice, and turning around.

"They run," said Hairs. "I would run from me too."

Fern's heart still said *danger.* It said it quietly now.
Her hands were already reaching for his hair.

---

## 12. The braid

"Hold this," said Fern, and Bee held it.

Over, under, over — the way you do.
Thorn by thorn the wood let go of him.
Grey silver rope, thick as a river,
a hundred years long and getting shorter.

Fern was scared the entire time. She braided anyway.

> *But scared is not the same as sure.*

---

## 13. Standing up

When the last thorn let go, Hairs stood.

He was even bigger standing.
He was even gentler standing.
The braid lay behind him through the whole Bramblewood,
shining, like a path somebody had finally finished.

"Oh," said Hairs, looking at his own feet. "There they are."

---

## 14. Three bowls

Supper was blackberries and brown bread
and something orange in a pot with a lid on.

Three bowls, already out. Already waiting.
Already a little bit dusty.

Bee ate with her hands. Fern ate two helpings.
Hairs mostly watched them eat,
which is its own kind of full.

---

## 15. The village comes to look

The village came to the thorn gate that night,
all of them, standing well back, being very brave together.

Hairs opened his mouth. **GRRRR,** said Hairs.

Everybody flinched.

"He said *please,*" said Fern. "He says it like that.
You get used to it."

And they were still scared, most of them, a little.

---

## 16. Thursdays

Now the Bramblewood has a path through it,
silver, and it needs re-braiding about once a month,
and Bee is in charge of holding the end.

Now there are more than three bowls.

And when somebody new comes down the path
and hears that sound and stops —
the village doesn't say *hungry* anymore.
The village says, "Keep walking. That's how he says hello."

> *But scared is not the same as sure.*

**The End — of the one Luna dreamed up.**
