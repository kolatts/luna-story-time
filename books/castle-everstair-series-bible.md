# Castle Everstair — series bible

Canon for the Castle Everstair stories. This file outranks any single book prompt. Append new canon here as books are written; never contradict it silently.

*(This file was reconstructed after Book Three was written — the bible referenced by the Book Two and Book Three prompts predates the repo. Everything below reflects the books as published plus the author notes that shaped them.)*

---

## The world

- **Castle Everstair** — a lavender fairy-tale castle whose great stair starts in the sea and climbs all the way to the moon. The moon climbs too.
- **The castle sea and the pool** — the sea begins where the bottom step ends. Cupped in the rocks is a warm round pool: "the sea with the wildness outside."
- **The dark tower** — across from the stair, where the moonlight goes thin. Home of the tower sisters. As of Book Three it has been *opened*: it grew taller overnight, sprouted a crown of doors, and kept **one new door it didn't take down**.
- **The stair's missing step** — near the ninety-ninth bend, one stone never came back after the un-stitching (Book Three). They hop it. It stays missing.
- **The moon's faint line** — the crack from Book Three healed, mostly. On very clear nights you can still find it.

## Rules of the world (never break these)

1. **Moon and Baby Lady are zero years old.** Born with the moon. This is a real fact, not a joke. They **never argue** — which is why the single time they snapped at each other (Book Six, drained and nap-robbed by the pot) it read as an alarm bell, not a quarrel. Restored by sleep; they matched again even dreaming. Any future argument between them must carry that same weight: it means something is deeply wrong.
2. **Cottontail fights with her claws, and borrowed magic always fails her.** She had no powers of her own before the last page of Book Three (in Book Two she briefly carried Moon's and Baby Lady's gifted shine, but it slid off her in the deep and went home; her claws did the saving). Any magic that is truly hers must *grow*, never be given — and at the very end of Book Three, her first tiny shine grew in, on the tip of one claw, seen by nobody.
3. **Dreams of hope** is Moon's favorite power and it can't be stolen, because it doesn't live inside her — it lives in other people.
4. **Shock's mask never comes off.** No part of her face is ever visible, on any page, in any book.
5. **What's under the mask is never stated**: a pretty face with a small pink blotch she can't remove. She hid her whole self behind something monstrous rather than let one mark be seen. This is *felt*, never said.
6. **The fifth sister was revealed in Book Six**: Leeblebeest, blue all over, the watcher who never slept. (Through Book Five she was only hinted — five beds, one uncounted voice.) There are no more hidden sisters; five is the whole set.
7. **The door never closes on Shock permanently.** Beaten back, changed, delayed, understood — never gone forever.
8. Danger stays bedtime-safe: the scary things are masks, silence, and doors. Nobody is hurt. Nothing is gory.
9. Voice: soft rhyme with strong cadence — **storytelling first, rhyme second**. 16 spreads. A new refrain each book, placed four times (spreads 6, 9, 12, 16: early, mid, at the turn, at the close).
10. Same painterly watercolor style, same indigo/lavender/gold palette, same crescent motif, every book. Character sheets are reused **verbatim**, never paraphrased.
11. **Art consistency, checked across every book's images during review** (not just per-image): the SKY moon is always round and full — in Book Three it carries a hairline crack from spread 5 until it heals by spread 14. Crescent moons appear only as the motif (crowns, masks, carvings, decor), never in the sky. Also check continuity of time of day, the tower's look (grim black stone), recurring props, and every character's sheet details from image to image. Elysian's skull is formed by her HAIR only (visible strands, like Book Four spread 6) — never a pattern on her robe; her robe is plain ragged black with no markings. No signature-like squiggles anywhere in the art.
12. **Narration heteronyms**: when a spread's verse uses a word whose pronunciation the voice gets wrong (e.g. past-tense "read"), add a `pronunciations` map to that spread in book.json (word → IPA, e.g. `{"read": "rɛd"}`); generate-narration.py applies it via SSML phoneme tags and keeps word-highlight timings in sync. Listen for heteronyms (read/lead/tear/wound/live/bow) when proofing narration.

## The family

Five sisters were born in the tower. As of Book Six, all five have been shown.

- **Shock** — the oldest, and the secret. Evilest and Beedlist did not know she existed until Book Three; she is "the secret the tower was built here to keep." Tall, storm-grey hair, charcoal cloak fading to mist, carved storm-creature mask with two small carved fangs at its frown, a hairline crack down one cheek and a tipped-sideways crescent scratched above the brow. Her voice is like snowfall. She wants all three sets of powers — Moon's, Baby Lady's, Cottontail's — to crumble the world into pieces and rebuild it evil; she believes the world deserves it. In Book Three she was *seen* (Moon looked at her dream and didn't look away), hated it, and left. Not beaten. Not finished. She paces the horizon. A bowl of soup stays set for her.
- **Evilest** — spiky black crown, loud, wants power. Softening arc: in Book Three she recognized Shock's empty rebuilt world as her own Book One dream (the throne room with nobody there) and said "I don't think I want to be queen, after all." Now sits at the soup table, arms crossed, staying.
- **Cheeblest** — bows and pigtails, the lonely one; turned good at the end of Book One and never went back. The only one who has stood on both sides. The tower never once opened its door for her the way it did for Shock. Carries Book Three: "Do the thing that you once did for me! Just LOOK!"
- **Beedlist** — the still one, all black, never speaks. Deep where Evilest is loud. Even the sea goes flat around her. Keeps a small black book with no name; in Book Three she "spoke" for the first time — three written words: *SHE WON'T STOP.* Now writes at the soup table between sips.
- **Leeblebeest** — the fifth and last sister, revealed in Book Six. Blue all over — face, hands, everything — eerie but never frightening. The watcher: while the others slept she "kept all the watch," and nobody ever let her rest either. Patient, unhurried, the mastermind type — she doesn't fight, she waits. Her little clay pot (Book Six) didn't steal power; it caught what leaked when the twins were tired and upset. Roots cracked it open from the inside; the shards blossomed in the garden. She was invited to lie down and "be a seed too" (by Cheeblest, who was forgiven once herself). Sat at the garden-party table, blinking in daylight. Her fifth little bed in the tower was kept made all along.

## The parents (as of Book Five)

- **Purple Shine** — a small all-purple cat, half fairy half mermaid (lavender wings, amethyst mermaid tail, no back legs — same "recipe" as Baby Lady). Moon and Baby Lady's **mama**. Came from the future with Pink Shine to find their daughters; the way back closed behind them and can never unclose. When she transforms she becomes poofy and normal-sized. Warm, watchful, steps in front of the twins before she thinks.
- **Pink Shine** — a fluffy rose-pink dog, half fairy half mermaid (pearl-pink wings, rose-gold mermaid tail, no back legs). The twins' **papa**. Woolly deep bark; started the everybody-song. Steady and gentle.
- They live at the castle now. The twins' half-fairy-half-mermaid nature comes from them; this was the clue hiding in plain sight all of Book Five.

## The friends

- **Princess Moon** — pink hair, gold wings, silver tail, crescent crown. Every power, but reaches for them less than she used to. Favorite: dreams of hope.
- **Baby Lady** — Moon's sister, a talking puppy with pearl wings and all the same powers. Everyone hears her — the fish and the hawk. **In art she has no back legs and no back paws, ever** — her puppy body ends in her mermaid tail; front paws only.
- **Baby Jag Cottontail** — a jaguar cub from "an island of somewheres," older than Moon and Baby Lady, arrived by driftwood log with a green-leaf sail. Ordinary claws, seashell bracelet. A friend, not a sister. Stayed after Book Two. As of the last page of Book Three, a first tiny shine of her own has grown in on the tip of one claw — nobody in the story knows yet.
- **Dirt** — a third jaguar cub (Book Six), Cottontail's "dirt friend." Soil-brown coat, leaf-green hair tuft in the same style as Cottontail's and Winds', deep whistling voice, wide slow smile. Not shy, doesn't hide — simply, joyfully, completely into dirt: gardens, soil, roots, worms, seeds. Sleeps in his garden beds. He already knew the series' quietest truth (sleep first, then flowers; the growing happens in the dark) before anyone else thought to ask. As of Book Six the twins gave him a power of his own: wherever Dirt sleeps, things grow. (He'd had it for years, if you asked the beans. Now it sparkles.)
- **Hairs** — an enormous, gentle, mountain-sized grey wolf with a hundred years of unbrushed silver hair trailing behind him. He says exactly one word — "GRRRR" — and it means *please*; he has been practicing it since before anybody was born and it keeps coming out wrong. He arrived in the world through **Hairs and the Growl That Meant Please** (a Dreamed Up By You book, dreamed up by Luna from Ohio: Princess Fern and Baby Bee braided him free of the Bramblewood thorns and the village learned to hear him). Book Seven brings him into the main series. Shy, hungry, agreeable, ashamed easily. His sheet is reused verbatim from that book and must never be paraphrased.
- **Poodle** — a small lean grey wolf with oddly tight-curled fur (hence the name), a tattered green scarf, and a scheme a minute. Book Seven. Does the planning and all of the talking, including "translating" what Hairs said into whatever Poodle wanted him to say. Cries when he is happy and would like that kept quiet.
- **Witchy** — a small round grandmotherly good witch from a crooked cottage at the edge of the grey woods. Patched blue cloak, floppy pointed hat, round spectacles, a fat glass bottle of swirling color. **Explicitly not Elysian** — the books say so on the page ("Not the witch under the bottom step. A different one. A kind one. There are kind ones."). She brewed the potion that gave the world its color back; her rule is that a tale is only half a tale — the other half is somebody listening.
- **Pearl** — the oldest mermaid of the sea, sea-foam hair, pale-green mother-of-pearl tail. When Pearl tells a tale, the sea holds still to hear it, and her story comes out as bubbles with color inside them. Book Seven establishes that mermaids other than the twins exist and come up from the deep for occasions.
- **Captain Pim and the 100 Guardians** — palm-sized silver moth-knights of the Stair. Book One only, so far. No cavalry since.

## The books

1. **Princess Moon and the Nevershine Lantern** — the trick. The black-glass lantern swallows the powers; hope can't be stolen because it isn't inside her. Refrain: *"But dreams slip through the smallest crack."* Ends with four bowls of soup.
2. **Baby Jag Cottontail and the Deep Dark Sea** — the theft. Beedlist gathers reflections off the water and the powers follow; the borrowed magic fails Cottontail and her own claws save everyone. Refrain: *"But the deep can't keep what was always yours."* Ends with five bowls (one "for anyone stormy who might not be there"), a reflection that waves a moment too late — and Shock, masked and silent, standing on the sea. Nobody fights her. Nobody speaks to her.
3. **Cheeblest and the Sister of Storms** — the bargain. The tower opens for Shock and locks its own sisters out; the sister reveal; the storm un-stitches the world (floating stair stones, a crack on the moon) against the price of all three shines; Evilest and Beedlist join her, then turn; Moon stops her by *seeing* her, not beating her. Refrain: *"Even storms were little once."* Ends with seven bowls — six drinkers and the stormy one's, full, set, waiting — the storm pacing the horizon all night, and, on the very last page, Cottontail's first shine flickering in on the tip of one claw while she sleeps — visible in the art and named in the text ("A shine of her own. Her very first one."). Grown, not given. No character saw; every reader did.

4. **Winds and the Witch of the Deep Cave** — the ink. Elysian, the family's secret aunt under the bottom step, brews out the twins' powers and paints Baby Lady "finished"; plain rain through the opened cave roof washes the ink away; the shineless jaguars (nose and claws) save everyone. Refrain: *"But nobody picks how they started."* Ends with the twins gifting the jaguars powers of their own — given, not borrowed — and the stormy bowl drunk from at dawn, by nobody anyone saw.

5. **Purple Shine and the Unicorn Who Screams** — the parents. Two strangers who already know everything arrive from the future: the twins' mama and papa, the way home closed behind them. A nameless black unicorn whose scream pushes people too far apart to hear each other tears the family away and reveals the truth as blackmail. The twins power up each with her own parent — purple crowns, all-gold wings and tails, necklaces unchanged; **crowns can't fight, but crowns can find**. She is beaten by the everybody-song: one loud warm sound with no apart in it and no quiet to tear at. Refrain: *"But the song always knows its way home."* Ends with the twins' birthday (they turn zero again), the parents' first one there — and, far out where the dark begins, the unicorn humming one bad note.

6. **Dirt and the Blue Sister's Pot** — the tiring. The fifth sister, Leeblebeest, revealed at last: blue all over, holding a little clay pot that steals nothing — it only catches what leaks when the twins are tired and upset. So nobody attacks; the sisters simply ruin every nap, drip by drip. The twins snap at each other for the first time ever; claws and sky find nothing to fight. Dirt, the garden jaguar, knows the thing: everyone lies down in the warm soil, tucked in like seeds, and sleeps — calm has no upset in it, and roots crack the pot open from the inside. The powers grow back the root way. Leeblebeest, who was never let rest either, is given a place in the garden by Cheeblest ("You get to be a seed too"). Refrain: *"But the growing happens while you sleep."* Art note: Book Six deliberately adds warm earth tones — soil browns, garden greens — against the moonlit palette. Ends with a garden party, every sister at one long table (the stormy one quiet at the end of the row, a soup bowl set before her), and Dirt gifted his own power.

7. **Poodle, Hairs, and the Mermaid's Tale** — the taking. The world has gone **grey** — all color drained out of everything, slowly, the way quiet comes; nobody says why (see open threads). Witchy's potion needs one last ingredient, *a mermaid's tale* — a story, not a tail — so she walks to Everstair, where the mermaids are surfacing for the twins' birthday. Two hungry wolves follow the smell and sweep the entire feast away in Hairs' mane. The theft costs nothing but joy, and joy turns out to be the half of the recipe that matters: Pearl keeps telling her tale to a table that has stopped leaning in, the bubbles grey out, and the bottle stops at halfway. Moon finds the wolves by reaching for dreams of hope and discovers they are dreaming of a table with them at it. Caught in the frosting, Hairs says his one word and Poodle mistranslates it on purpose; Moon doesn't. "He's asking to be asked." The feast comes home, the party leans back in, the bottle fills, and Witchy pours color over the whole world from the top step. Refrain: *"But joy is not a thing you can carry."* Art note: spreads 1–14 are deliberately desaturated to soft greys — gold lamplight, the full moon and the bottle stay true — and spreads 15–16 go to full saturated color; the contrast is the point. Ends with both wolves at the table with bowls of their own, the stormy bowl still set at the end — and one small patch of the world, far out where the dark begins, still grey, with something humming one note badly.

## Open threads (doors left open on purpose)

- **The deep kept a copy.** Moon's reflection waved back a moment too late (Book Two) and no book has explained it. Something is still down there.
- ~~**Five little beds**~~ — *paid off in Book Six.* The fifth bed was Leeblebeest's; the sisters kept it made all along ("You kept mine made. That was kind.").
- **Beedlist's nameless black book.** What she writes in it has never been shown, except once.
- **The tower's one kept door** — the new door it didn't take down when it shrank back.
- **The stormy bowl** — set at every meal, never yet claimed. If Shock ever knocks, this is how she comes in.
- **The missing step and the moon's faint line** — the world remembers the storm.
- **Shock is still out there**, pacing the horizon like someone who almost knocks.
- **Cottontail's shine** — it began on the last page of Book Three and nobody in the story has seen it yet. Shock came for a power that didn't exist; now it does. As of Book Four the jaguars also carry given powers of their own.
- **The unicorn is still out there.** Her scream faded to nothing against the everybody-song and she walked back down the bare sea road — but far out where the dark begins she hummed one note, badly. Moon's invitation ("There's always room in a song this size") stands. She never said her name.
- **Shock sang.** In Book Five a voice soft as snowfall joined the everybody-song from behind, and nobody turned; she stood at the birthday doorway, present and unremarked, and the stormy bowl was drunk from early. Nobody has yet spoken to her.
- ~~**The voice nobody placed**~~ — *paid off in Book Six:* the uncounted voice in the everybody-song was Leeblebeest, watching as always. (Never stated on the page; the reveal makes it legible on reread.)
- **Leeblebeest's watch** — "Somebody had to stay up, keeping count." Keeping count of *what*, and for whom, has never been said. She watched every book happen. What she saw, no book has told.
- **The blossoming shards** — the little pot broke from the inside and its pieces sprouted gold flowers in the castle garden. Nobody has picked them.
- **The future the Shines came from** — they traded a whole world for their girls and it can never unclose. What world? No book has said.

- **The Grey.** Nobody in Book Seven says where it came from or why it started after Book Six. Witchy's pour brought it all back *except one small far-off patch where the dark begins* — the same place the unicorn walked off to, humming badly. Never stated. Left for whoever wants it.
- **Books can cross over.** Book Seven pulled Hairs out of a Dreamed Up By You book and into the main series. Reader-dreamed characters are canon-eligible; their sheets are reused verbatim like anyone else's.
