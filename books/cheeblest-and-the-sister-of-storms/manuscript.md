# Cheeblest and the Sister of Storms

*Book Three of the Castle Everstair stories*

---

## Liberties I took

You said decide, so here they are in one place — easy to overrule:

- **The title is *Cheeblest and the Sister of Storms*** — Cheeblest earns the cover of this one. She's the only person in the world who has stood on both sides of it.
- **The tower opened.** It grew taller overnight, sprouted a crown of doors, let the masked stranger walk straight in — and locked its own sisters out on the sand. Doors don't open for strangers. That's how everyone knows, before anyone says it, that she isn't one.
- **The reveal:** Shock is the sisters' secret older sister. Evilest and Beedlist had no idea. Evilest joins her on the spot (a bigger, more terrible sister — her dream come true), then turns when she sees the world Shock would build and recognizes it: her own lonely dream from Book One, the throne room with nobody in it.
- **Beedlist speaks for the first time** — in writing. Three words held up in her nameless black book: *SHE WON'T STOP.*
- **The storm bargain:** Shock un-stitches the world, gently — stair stones float up like puzzle pieces, a hairline crack crosses the moon — and offers to stop when the three shines are brought to her. Nobody is hurt. Nothing falls. The quiet of it is the scary part.
- **The ending is seeing, not beating.** Moon can't take the mask off, and doesn't try. She looks at Shock's dream with dreams of hope, and what she finds is folded up small like a letter: somebody looking at her, and then not looking away. Shock hates being seen, and leaves. "Not beaten. Not finished. Not sorry. Not done — just gone for tonight, like a storm. Or a sun."
- **What the mask hides is never said.** Not stated, not shown, not hinted at on the page. The dream-vision keeps the girl in silhouette.
- **The crumbling is small and reversible** — except one stair step that never comes back (they hop the gap; it's how they remember that somewhere, storms are) and a faint healed line on the moon. The tower keeps one new door it didn't take down.
- **Five little beds**, spread 9, in a small dusty room. Nobody counts them. That's on purpose.
- **The refrain:** *"Even storms were little once."* It appears four times — early, mid, at the turn, at the close.
- **The last spread:** the stormy bowl stays set, and the storm paces the horizon like someone outside a door who almost knocks. Moon waves at it, like you do. It doesn't wave back. It stays all night.
- **Cottontail's shine** — on the very last page, asleep in her chair, a tiny shine flickers on the tip of one claw. Not borrowed, not gifted: grown, the way her claws grew. Nobody in the story sees it. (This is Book Four's door: Shock came for a power that didn't exist. Now it does.)

## The manuscript — 16 spreads

### Spread 1 — The silence

Nobody spoke when the summer was done.
Nobody spoke — not a single one.
For out on the water, as still as a stone,
stood somebody masked, and she stood all alone.

She looked at them once through the holes in her mask.
She didn't want soup. She had nothing to ask.
She turned, and she walked on the sea like a floor,
and the tower — their tower — swung open its door.

### Spread 2 — Locked out

By morning the tower was taller. It's true.
And it hadn't asked anyone. Towers don't do
the things this one did: it had grown, overnight,
a crown of new doors at a dizzying height.

And down on the sand stood the sisters, locked out —
locked out of the home they'd been scary about.
Evilest hammered and hollered, "IT'S ME!"
The door didn't budge. It was waiting, you see.

### Spread 3 — Doors don't open for strangers

Across the wide water the friends saw it all:
the mask on the sea, and the tower grown tall.
"Who is she?" asked Moon. Cottontail didn't know:
"I've sailed every somewhere a sailboat can go,

and nobody, nowhere, wears masks on the sea."
Then Cheeblest said, quietly, "Listen to me.
Doors don't open for strangers. It knew her. It did.
I lived my whole life there —

and it never swung open like that once for me."

### Spread 4 — The secret the tower kept

Inside, up a stairway as new as the day,
the sisters found halls that had not been that way,
and there stood the masked one, so still and so tall,
and her voice, when it came, was like snow. That was all.

"Sisters," she said. "I'm your sister. I'm Shock.
I was born in this tower. I'm older than talk.
And nobody told you. Not once. Not a peep.
I'm the secret this tower was built here to keep."

And Evilest lit up spike after spike:
a BIGGER, more terrible sister! Her like!
She bowed till her crown nearly swept at the floor:
"Whatever you're planning — I'm yours. Tell me more."

But Beedlist said nothing. She stayed in the black.
She looked at the mask. And the mask looked back.

### Spread 5 — The un-stitching

Then Shock climbed her tower and stood at the crown,
and the sky went the grey of a storm sitting down,
and she reached up and pinched, very gently, like thread —
and a stone of the stair floated up from its bed.

Then two stones. Then ten. Like puzzle-bits, slow,
they drifted up out of the place stones should go.
And thin as an eyelash, far up past the blue,
a crack crossed the moon. And the crack was new.

"I'll stop," said the mask, in her snowfall voice,
"when you bring me the shines. All three. It's a choice.
This world came out wrong. I will take it apart,
and build a new world in the shape of my heart."

### Spread 6 — Coming loose

How do you fight with the weather? You don't.
You can ask it to stop, but it probably won't.
They watched their dear stair come undone, bit by bit,
like somebody slowly unbuttoning it.

The stones didn't fall. Nobody was hurt.
They rose, soft as bubbles, away from the dirt,
and they hung in the sky, and they didn't make sound.
And a quiet that's wrong is the loudest thing round.

*Even storms were little once.*

### Spread 7 — The storm and the cat

Then the storm came for Cottontail's power. It pried.
It circled her, sniffing. It hunted. It tried.
It turned out her pockets — no pockets. It blew
through her fur and her whiskers. No magic came through.

"I keep telling everyone," said the wet cat,
"there isn't a shine under this, under that.
My claws are just claws. They grew where they stay.
You can't take a thing that won't come away."

And the storm went quite still, for it didn't know what
to do with a something that couldn't be got.

### Spread 8 — The givers

But Moon and her sister sat close on the stair,
by the space where a step had gone off in the air,
and they thought the same thought, and they thought it as one
(they have never once argued — not ever, not yet):
"She can have every shine. She can have every sun.
A world full of friends is the best you can get."

So they filled up their arms with their shimmer and glow
and set off for the tower to give it away.
And the storm, up above, watched them come. And you know
that a storm shouldn't smile. But it did, in its grey.

### Spread 9 — The world she would build

Meanwhile the sisters, inside, on their own,
went poking through hallways the tower had grown,
past a small dusty room where five little beds slept,
all made, very neat, like a promise half-kept,

to a door standing open a crack, silver-grey,
with the world Shock would build when this world went away:
it was tidy. It shone. It was rowed. And it gleamed.
And Evilest stopped. For she knew it. She'd dreamed:

a room full of crowns and a very tall chair,
and nobody, nobody, nobody there.
She looked a long look. Then she said, very small,
"I don't think I want to be queen, after all."

*Even storms were little once.*

### Spread 10 — She won't stop

They ran. Down the stairways, down two at a time,
out the door, down the sand, to the edge of the brine,
and stood in the path of the glow coming near.
"STOP," puffed out Evilest. "Keep your shine here.

She's our sister. Our oldest. And nobody knew.
Not us. Not our whole lives. I promise it's true.
And I've seen the new world she would build when it's done —
there's nobody in it. Not us. Not anyone."

And Beedlist, who never says anything, wrote
three small words in her book, and held up the note,
and the three words were these, in her smallest black pen:
"SHE WON'T STOP." And the sea went flat again.

### Spread 11 — The girl who knew towers

Then Cheeblest walked forward, alone, up the sand,
past sisters, past friends, holding nobody's hand,
and she stood at the foot of the tower, head high,
in her bows, very small, looking up at the sky,

and she called to the mask at the top of it all:
"I know about towers! I lived in one too!
You hide till the hiding is all that you do!

I had a small dream and I hid it away —
and somebody looked at it anyway. SEE?
Moon! Do the thing that you once did for me!
Don't run! Don't give! Don't fight her! Just LOOK!"

### Spread 12 — The looking

So Moon set her armful of shine on the sand,
and took Baby Lady's soft paw in her hand,
and she closed her eyes gently, and looked the long way —
past the mask, past the storm, past the grey, past the grey.

And deep at the middle of all of that weather
she found one small dream, folded up like a letter,
and the dream wasn't thunder, and it wasn't rain,
and it wasn't a world crumbled small. It was plain:

somebody looking at her, and then —
not looking away. Just looking again.

*Even storms were little once.*

### Spread 13 — Not beaten, not finished

And high on the tower, the storm lost its spin,
and the stones hung as still as the night they were in.
"Stop looking at me," said the mask, low and thin.
And Moon said, quite gently, "I already did.

I can't see your face, and I won't even try.
But I saw you. I saw you. You're not very hid."

And Shock — who could crumble a world into sand —
took one step away, like a wave leaves the land,
and stepped through a door that was suddenly there,
and the door closed behind her. And storm became air.

Not beaten. Not finished. Not sorry. Not done —
just gone for tonight, like a storm. Or a sun.

### Spread 14 — The missing step

The stones settled home like slow flakes of snow,
each back into place in the stair's even row —
all but one step, near the ninety-ninth bend,
that never came down. There's a gap there, my friend.
They hop it. You'd hop it. It isn't so far.
But it's how they remember that somewhere, storms are.

And the crack on the moon knitted shut, thin and slow,
the way that a scrape on your knee learns to go —
though on some very clear nights, you can find a faint line,
like the moon has a secret. (It does. It's fine.)

And the tower shrank back to its everyday frown,
and it kept one new door that it didn't take down.

### Spread 15 — Soup, crowded

That night at the table there wasn't much room.
Two extras had followed them home through the gloom:
one girl with a crown, sitting cross-armed and proud,
who said that the soup was "just fine" — but quite loud;

one girl all in black, who said nothing at all,
who wrote in her book with her back to the wall,
one word at a time, very neat, in between
small sips of her soup. (What it said, we've not seen.)

And the bowl for the stormy one? Full. Set. Still there,
with its steam going up by the one empty chair.
Nobody talked of it. Everyone knew.
Some bowls you keep setting. It's just what you do.

### Spread 16 — The storm on the horizon

And later, at bedtime, when lamps were all low,
Moon looked from her window, out where the waves go,
and there on the line where the sky meets the foam,
a little grey storm paced. It didn't come home.

It didn't come closer. It didn't quite go.
It walked the horizon, slow, to and fro,
the way you might pace at a door in the night
when you almost could knock. When you can't. When you might.

And Moon raised her hand to the glass. And it's true:
she waved at the storm. Like you do. Like you do.
The storm didn't wave back. (A storm has no hand.)
But it stopped its slow pacing, out there past the land,

and it stayed, very still, all the rest of the night.

And down in the warm room, asleep in her chair,
the jaguar was dreaming of salt and sea air,
and out on the tip of one ordinary claw,
a tiny shine flickered that nobody saw.

Not borrowed. Not gifted. Not stolen. Not new.
It grew there, the way that her claws had grown too.

*Even storms were little once.*

**The End** — *of the third one.*

## Art direction

### Global style string
> Painterly children's storybook illustration, soft watercolor and colored-pencil texture, visible brushwork, warm golden lamplight against deep indigo night, lavender and pearl-grey stone, generous sparkle and stardust trails, big expressive eyes, gentle rounded shapes, no harsh lines, no photorealism, no text in image.

### Character sheet (repeat verbatim in every prompt)
- **Princess Moon** — young girl, long wavy pink hair, large dark-blue eyes, rosy cheeks; delicate translucent golden fairy wings with fine filigree veining; silver iridescent mermaid tail with pearl-sheen scales; small gold crown topped with a crescent moon; pearl necklace with a gold crescent pendant.
- **Baby Lady** — small fluffy cocker-spaniel puppy, golden-cream curly ears, dark round eyes; iridescent pearl-white fairy wings, smaller and softer than Moon's; pale silver-pink mermaid tail in place of back legs — she has no back legs and no back paws, her puppy body ends in the mermaid tail, front paws only; tiny gold crescent charm on a pearl collar.
- **Baby Jag Cottontail** — young jaguar cub, soft yellow-gold coat with black rosette spots, a tuft of bright pink wavy hair, warm amber eyes, sturdy ordinary paws with small curved claws; no wings and no mermaid tail; a woven seashell bracelet on one front leg.
- **Evilest** — pale girl, black hair, tall spiky black crown, high-collared black gown, arms crossed, sharp confident smile.
- **Cheeblest** — pale girl, black hair in two bows and pigtails, deep-purple dress, watchful rather than cruel, eyes slightly too soft for the tower.
- **Beedlist** — pale girl dressed entirely in black: long straight black hair, plain long black high-collared dress, black shoes, black ribbon; no crown; no color anywhere on her; small calm unreadable face; perfectly still posture, hands folded in front of her.
- **Shock** *(new)* — a tall slender girl, a head taller than the other sisters, always standing very straight; long storm-grey hair falling past her waist; a long charcoal-grey hooded cloak whose hem fades into soft mist; grey gloves; her whole face hidden behind a smooth dark carved mask shaped like a gently scowling storm-cloud creature with two small carved fangs at its frown, round dark eye-holes, a thin hairline crack down one cheek, and a small tipped-sideways crescent moon scratched above the brow; the mask never comes off and no part of her face is ever visible; unsettling but gentle, never gory or horrific.
- **Recurring motif** — the crescent moon, plus (new this book) doors in places doors don't belong.

Full bright moon, calm sea, hushed.
2. Soft grey morning on wet black sand: the dark stone tower grown strangely taller overnight, sprouting a crown of many small mismatched doors where its windows used to be; at its base Evilest hammering on the big shut main door, crown askew, mid-holler, while Beedlist stands perfectly still beside her, hands folded. Silver morning light, unsettling but gentle.
3. Morning at the warm rock pool: Moon, Baby Lady and the young jaguar cub gathered close around Cheeblest, who looks out across the wide grey-blue sea toward the distant grim black stone tower silhouetted dark against the sky, its many new doors faintly visible; worried small faces, warm golden light from the castle behind them, the pool glowing gently.
4. Inside the tower, a vast new hall of pale grey stone lit warmly by tall candelabras: the tall masked sister standing very straight at the center; Evilest delighted, grinning wide, mid-bow with her spiky crown nearly sweeping the floor; Beedlist far back in soft shadow, perfectly still, watching the mask. Unsettling but gentle, warm candlelight against grey stone.
5. The tall masked sister completely alone — no other people or children anywhere in the scene — standing at the very top of the tower beneath a huge, slow, soft grey storm-swirl; far across the water, glowing golden stones of the great castle stair drifting gently up into the sky like floating puzzle pieces; high above, a thin hairline crack across the bright full moon. The scene stays luminous: moonlight, warm stair-glow, soft violet sky.
6. Seen from the castle side: Moon, Baby Lady, the young jaguar cub and Cheeblest small at the foot of the great stair as several of its glowing stones float calmly upward around them like golden bubbles; the children holding each other and gazing up; grey sea, heavy soft sky, everything lit warm gold by the floating stones.
7. On the wet sand in the wind: the young jaguar cub standing firm and unbothered as soft ribbons of grey storm-wind curl all around her, ruffling her fur and pink hair and finding nothing to take; she holds up one paw showing small ordinary claws, her face calm and almost amused; warm moonlight breaking through the grey. No other animals.
8. Night: Moon and Baby Lady walking together along the shore toward the distant tower, arms and paws full of glowing gold, silver and pink light, faces gentle and determined; ahead, the grim black stone tower, its big door slowly opening; grey storm sky above, the carried light glowing warm around them like lanterns.
9. Inside the tower: Evilest and Beedlist at a tall grey doorway glowing silver, looking into a dream-like vision of a perfectly tidy, shining, empty world — neat rows of crowns and one very tall empty throne, beautiful and cold, nobody in it; Evilest's face fallen and small; behind them in the dim candlelit hallway, a small dusty room with exactly five little neat black beds in a row just visible — black wooden bed frames, black blankets — five beds, no more. Unsettling but gentle.
10. On the wet black shore before the tower door: Evilest with arms flung wide, blocking the path of Moon and Baby Lady who carry armfuls of glowing light; beside her Beedlist holds up a small open black book, its page showing only tiny unreadable pen marks; urgent but tender faces, storm-grey sky, the children lit warm by the carried glow.
11. Small brave Cheeblest standing alone at the foot of the huge tower, bows and purple dress tugged sideways by the wind, head high, calling up toward the tiny masked figure at the tower's distant top far above — Cheeblest is completely alone on the shore, no other people or children anywhere in the scene except the tiny masked figure far above; grey storm light warmed by gold from the castle far across the sea.
12. Dream-vision spread with soft dissolving edges, framed in swirling gentle storm-grey clouds: inside a hushed grey room where all the mirrors are draped with white sheets, a small girl seen only from behind as a soft dark silhouette, face never visible, standing before a door open just a crack with warm golden light spilling through toward her. Tender, hushed, glowing, kind.
13. The storm holding its breath at first pale gold of dawn: floating golden stair stones hanging motionless in the sky; on the tower top, the tall masked sister stepping backward through a tall grey freestanding door, her cloak trailing into mist, the door half closed behind her; far below, Moon watches gently with one hand slightly raised. Quiet, soft, luminous.
14. Soft pink dawn: the great glowing stair rebuilt as the last golden stones settle down into place like slow snowflakes — one single gap where a step is missing, and Moon, Baby Lady, the young jaguar cub and Cheeblest all hopping over the gap together mid-step, laughing; above, the fading moon with the faintest thin healed line across its face. Joyful, warm, light.
15. Cozy warm interior at night: a round wooden table crowded with seven bowls of steaming soup under golden lamplight — Moon, Baby Lady, Cheeblest and the young jaguar cub squeezed in happily, Evilest — a little girl the same size and age as the other children, not an adult — seated with arms crossed but staying, and Beedlist, also a small girl, writing in a small black book between sips; every character is a small child — and one empty chair with a full steaming bowl waiting in front of it; crescent moon carved above the door.
16. Quiet night, seen from inside a warm dim candlelit room: Moon at a tall arched castle window, one hand raised to the glass in a small wave toward the sea — and far out on the moonlit horizon, a small soft grey storm-swirl standing perfectly still on the water. Hushed, tender, silver sea, one warm candle.

**Cover:** Cheeblest small and brave in the foreground, standing on the great glowing castle stair with her purple dress and black bows caught in the wind, looking out across a deep dark sea at a vast soft grey storm swirling above the distant tall black tower — and faint within the storm-swirl, the barest suggestion of a dark mask shape; warm gold light from the castle windows behind her. Open sky in the upper third for the title.
