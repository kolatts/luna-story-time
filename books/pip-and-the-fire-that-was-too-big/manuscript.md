# Pip and the Fire That Was Too Big

*Dreamed up by Harper*

A Dreamed Up By You standalone. Ages 4–8. Sixteen spreads.

**Refrain:** But a fire has to go somewhere.

---

## 1. The last hill

On the last hill before the mountains,
where the road gets tired and gives up,
Nell and Bo stack the woodpile before supper.

Not for tonight. Tonight is only a Tuesday.
They stack it for the seventh night —

because every seventh sunset the whole sky goes pink,
and two specks in all that pink come flying.

---

## 2. Plum and Pip

Down over the ridge they come, wings wide.

Plum first: purple as a plum, bigger than the barn,
puffing small silver smoke rings out of his nose.

Then Pip, rose-pink, all wings and elbows,
who lands the way a leaf lands
if the leaf were extremely excited.

"HELLO," says Bo, who is four, and means it.

---

## 3. The lighting of the fire

This is how it goes, every seventh night.

The wood is stacked. Everybody sits.
Plum leans down and breathes one thin gold thread —

and the woodpile catches,
and the hill goes warm and orange,
and the mountains stand around in the dark
like grown-ups listening at a door.

---

## 4. The one who never lights it

Pip sits farther back than the others.
Pip always sits farther back.

She holds her breath a lot, Pip does.
She laughs with her mouth shut,
which is a very hard way to laugh.

And she has never, not once, lit the fire.

Bo has asked her nine times why.

---

## 5. The spring before

Here is why.

Last spring Pip laughed. Really laughed — out loud,
the whole entire laugh —
and the fire came out with it.

Not a thread. A ROAR of a thing.
It ate the old swing and half the great oak
and left the sky smelling of smoke for a week.

Nobody was hurt.
Everybody was scared.

---

## 6. Smaller, Pip

After that, the village had a word for Pip.
*Too much,* they said. Too much, too big, too hot.

"Smaller, Pip," said the village.
"Smaller," said Pip, and got smaller,
and swallowed every laugh she had for a year.

But swallowed fire doesn't go out.
It only goes quiet.

---

## 7. The sunset that didn't behave

Then came a seventh sunset that didn't behave.

The wind swung round cold off the mountains.
Grey came over the pink and pressed it flat.
Rain fell sideways and mean, and then fog —
thick as porridge, filling the valley to the brim.

The woodpile drank it all and sat there, soaked.

---

## 8. One short

Bo counted the lights down in the valley,
the way he does, every night, twice.

"One short," said Bo.

The wagon. The one that comes over the mountain road
with Gran and the flour and everybody's letters.
Out in that fog, with no light and no path
and a long drop on the left the whole way home.

---

## 9. What Plum's fire is for

The old iron beacon has stood on that hill forever,
rusty and empty, with nobody's fire in it.

Plum stretched up tall
and breathed his thin gold thread into the soaked black wood.

*Hiss,* said the wood. And nothing. And *hiss.*

Plum's fire is a sitting-close fire.
It is a good fire.
It was not the fire this needed.

---

## 10. Nell turns around

Nell stood still for one whole second.

Then she turned around and looked at the back of the hill,
where Pip was sitting, being small.

"Pip," said Nell. "We need the big one."

---

## 11. The size of a mouse

"You saw what it does," said Pip.
Her voice came out the size of a mouse.
"You saw the oak. You saw everybody's face."

"I did," said Nell. "I saw all of it."

And Bo took off his red hat, which he never does,
and set it on the wall, and said:
"We'll stand back. That's all it needs. Room."

---

## 12. The way big things need doing

So they did it properly,
the way big things need doing.

Nell walked Bo behind the old stone wall.
Plum lay down along the wall like a purple hill
and put one wing over both children, which is a roof.

"Now," called Nell, from under the wing.
"As big as you actually are."

---

## 13. The second sunset

Pip breathed in.

She breathed in the cold and the fog and the whole grey year,
and then she let go of it — all of it, the entire laugh —

and fire went up off that hill like the sun coming back.

The beacon caught.
The fog turned gold and got out of the way.
The hill was so bright the mountains had shadows again.

---

## 14. Seen from far away

Out on the mountain road, in all that white nothing,
Gran saw a gold place in the sky where the hill should be.

"There," said Gran, and turned the horses toward it.

And down in the valley the lanterns came out,
one and one and one,
every single one of them pointing the same way.

---

## 15. Nobody says too much

They came up the hill in the beacon's warmth —
Gran and the wagon and half the village behind her,
all of them lit orange, all of them looking up.

Nobody said *too much.*

Somebody said thank you. Then everybody did.

Pip put a wing over her face,
because that is a lot of being looked at.

---

## 16. Two fires

Now there are two fires on the last hill.

The little one is Plum's, for sitting close:
for stories, and burnt bread, and leaning on a dragon.

The big one is Pip's, up in the iron basket,
for the nights when somebody out there needs finding.

She lights it every seventh sunset now, rain or no rain.
And she laughs the whole entire laugh while she does it.

*The End — of the one Harper dreamed up.*
