# Princess Moon and the Purse of Maybes

*Book Eight of the Castle Everstair stories*
*Dreamed up by Luna. Written with love by Luna and Sunny.*

**Refrain:** *But you don't have to match to belong.* — placed on spreads 6, 9, 12, 16.

**Moral:** wanting everyone to be the same is really wanting one person to stand beside you. Belonging is being chosen, not being matched. (For the grown-up: the fort that swallows the world is loneliness that hasn't found the smaller ask underneath it.)

## Liberties taken (and canon honored)

- **Retcon:** the fifth sister's name is **Blooey** in every book, including Six and Seven. "Leeblebeest" no longer exists anywhere on the page. The internal character id stays `leeblebeest` for asset and voice continuity.
- The purse belongs to Princess Moon and Baby Lady. It came from the future with the Shines ("the only thing we brought"), which touches the open thread about their lost world without resolving it. It retrieves things that aren't real, plays as a guitar and as a violin, and changes the color of whatever it taps.
- Blooey tries **twice** and gets nothing either time. Try one: bubbles at bathtime, beaten by the purse-guitar. Try two: the bubble that doesn't pop swallows the castle and turns everything blue, and her own blue hides the very gold her bubbles catch.
- "The witch is still evil": the villain's helper is **Elysian**, the witch under the bottom step (Book Four), not Witchy. She brews the evil bubbles and goes back under the step exactly as evil as before. Witchy is only referred to (her pour in Book Seven).
- The fort is meant to replace the whole earth, and it nearly does; the blue reaches the tower, the wolves, and **the one grey place** from Book Seven, which then gets its color back at the end. That closes the Grey thread. The unicorn's hum is "nearly, nearly right" (still open).
- **Baby Lady saves the day**: she hears what Blooey actually wants, taps herself blue on purpose, and shows her that one is plenty. The fort, with nothing left to keep out, lets go.
- Moon uses dreams of hope once (spread 7) and says a soft no. Cottontail's secret claw-shine is not seen (still open). Shock stays on the horizon; the stormy bowl is set.
- Nobody argues, nobody is hurt. The scary things are a bubble, a witch who mutters, and a lot of blue.

## Art direction, whole book

Spreads 1–7 and 14–16: the usual indigo/lavender/gold palette. Spreads 8–13 are the **blue wash**: everything inside the fort, characters included, tinted soft blues (sky-blue, powder-blue, deep midnight-blue), with only the purse's clasp keeping a faint gold glint. Sky moon always perfectly round (blue-tinted in 8–13, never a crescent). Baby Lady goes deliberately all-over blue in spreads 12–14. Blooey is blue all along, so inside the fort she finally "matches" and is hard to pick out — that is the point of spread 12's composition, where the small blue puppy is the only thing she can see. Elysian's skull is in her hair only; robe plain. Cottontail is always "the young jaguar cub"; no rabbits.

## 1. The one grey place

Far out, where the dark begins, past the last of the sea,
there's a patch that stayed grey when the color came back.
Witchy poured. The whole world drank. But that one place — no.
Grey grass. Grey sky. Grey stones in a row.
And something out there hums one note, and hums it wrong.

But up at the castle the morning was pink,
and the stair was gold, and the sea was as blue as blue,
and on the twins' pillow, where nothing had been,
sat a purse.

A small, round, ordinary purse,
with a strap and a clasp and a shine on the clasp.
"Whose is it?" said Moon.
"Yours," said Purple Shine, from the doorway,
in the voice she keeps for very old things.
"It came with us. All the way. We were saving it."

*Art:* Split composition. Top corner: the far grey patch, a tiny desaturated pocket of grey grass and stones at the horizon where the dark begins, a faint hum-line of grey mist. Main scene: the twins' bedroom at Castle Everstair at pink morning, lavender stone, gold light through a round window, the great stair visible outside climbing to a perfectly round full moon still faint in the dawn sky. On a pillow, a small round lavender-and-gold purse with a shining clasp. Moon and Baby Lady lean over it, wide-eyed. Purple Shine hovers in the doorway, gentle and watchful.

## 2. What the purse can do

It's a plain-looking purse. It is not a plain purse.

Reach in, and your hand finds things that aren't real:
a ladder to a cloud that nobody's built,
a kite the color of Tuesday,
a song that hasn't been sung yet, still folded up small.
Baby Lady reached in and pulled out a maybe.
(It squeaked. She kept it.)

Strap it across you and strum, and it's a guitar.
Tuck it under your chin, and it's a violin.
And tap a thing with it, softly,
and the thing turns whatever color you were thinking of.
Moon tapped a rock. The rock went pink.
Baby Lady tapped Moon. Moon went pinker.

"Where we come from," said Pink Shine, gently,
"most things weren't real. So we needed somewhere to keep them."

*Art:* The castle terrace by the pool in morning light. Moon has pulled a tiny glowing ladder out of the purse that leads up into a cloud that is only half painted-in; a kite of an impossible shifting color floats above; a small folded-up song drifts out as a curl of gold notes. Baby Lady holds a small fuzzy round glowing "maybe" creature-shape in her front paws (a soft glowing puff with no face, not an animal). A rock on the terrace has just turned bright pink. Pink Shine watches from the pool's edge. Round full moon faint in the day sky.

## 3. Blue on grey

Out in the one grey place, somebody stood
who was the only color for miles.

Blue. All-over blue. Blue as the hour before morning.
Blooey. The fifth one. The one who kept count.
And beside her, hunched like a hook in the grey,
the witch from under the bottom step —
Elysian, one hundred and five, and every bit as evil as before,
stirring a cauldron that glowed a sick green,
and blowing, through a bent black straw,
bubbles.

Blue bubbles. Slow ones. The kind that don't pop.
"Everything they touch," said the witch, "goes blue.
And a shine that gets caught in one floats off. To you."

Blooey watched a bubble drift over a grey stone.
The stone went blue. It matched her.
"Good," she said. "Now: bigger."

*Art:* The one grey place: a wide desaturated grey meadow with grey stones in a row and a grey sky, far from the castle (castle a distant warm-gold speck on the horizon). In the middle of all the grey, the only color: Blooey, small and blue, standing calm, and one grey stone at her feet just turned bright blue. Beside her, Elysian hunched over a round black cauldron glowing sick green, blowing large slow blue bubbles through a bent black straw. A few blue bubbles drift up into the grey sky. Round full moon, pale grey, in the sky. No other people, no animals.

## 4. The project

"A fort," said Blooey. "That's the project.
Not a small one. Not a pillow one.
A fort so big it goes over the sea and the stair and the moon,
over the woods and the wolves and the witch,
and then there's no outside left at all.
Just inside. Just blue. Just everybody matching."

"Why?" said the witch, who only asked so she'd know where to stand.
Blooey turned a bubble slowly in her hands.
"Because I'm the only one," she said, "and I've been counting.
Five sisters. One blue. Have you ever tried to be the one?"

"To grow it that big, you'll need shine," said the witch.
"Their shine. Both twins. All of it."
"Then we'll go and get it," said Blooey. "Twice, if we have to.
I'm patient. I've had practice."

*Art:* Close on Blooey in the grey place, cupping one large slow blue bubble in both hands like a crystal ball; inside the bubble a tiny reflected dream-image of a whole blue world, a blue castle, blue stair, blue sea, blue moon. Elysian looms behind her at the cauldron, thin black wand raised, her long black hair falling into the faint shape of a skull (hair strands only). Grey meadow, grey sky, a scatter of blue bubbles beginning to rise. No other people, no animals.

## 5. First try

The bubbles came up the stair at bathtime,
which is the best time for bubbles, and Blooey knew it.
They came slow, and low, and blue, and lovely,
and the twins laughed and popped them, the way you do —

Pop! went a bubble on the tip of Moon's wing,
and a gold spark jumped in, and the bubble closed back up,
and floated off down the stair with a shine inside it.
Pop! on Baby Lady's nose. Another spark. Another bubble.
And where the bubbles had touched, the castle went blue:
a blue step. A blue towel. A blue bit of ceiling.

"Wait," said Moon. "Those are taking."
"Those are ours," said Baby Lady, and her ears went flat.

*Art:* A cozy castle bathroom at night, lavender stone, a round tub full of warm lamplit water and ordinary white foam. Big slow blue bubbles drift in through an arched window from the stair outside. One blue bubble is popping on the tip of Moon's golden wing and a small gold spark is being sucked inside another closing bubble; another bubble pops on Baby Lady's nose. Patches of the room have turned blue where bubbles touched: one blue step, a blue towel on a hook, a blue patch of ceiling. Moon's face turning from delight to alarm; Baby Lady's ears flat. Through the window, the round full moon.

## 6. The guitar

Moon grabbed the purse and slung it across her,
and (she'd never done this) she strummed.

It turned into a guitar the moment she touched it,
and the note that came out was the color of morning,
and every blue bubble that heard it went POP,
pop pop pop pop, like rain going upward,
and the sparks came home, the whole shining set,
and Baby Lady tapped the blue step gold again,
and the towel, and the ceiling, and, for good measure, the cat.
(Purple Shine was already purple. She didn't mind.)

Far below, in the one grey place, Blooey listened,
and counted the bubbles that came back empty.
"One," she said, and did not seem sad.
"That was one."

*Art:* Moon on the castle stair at night with the purse slung across her, strap over one shoulder, strumming it like a small guitar; a wave of warm gold-and-pink morning-colored music-light ripples out from it, and dozens of blue bubbles are popping mid-air in a cascade, each releasing a small gold spark that streams back toward the twins. Baby Lady, wings out, taps a blue step with her nose and it flushes back to lavender-gold. Purple Shine sits nearby, amused. Round full moon above the stair.

## 7. The ask

The second time, she came herself.
Blooey always does. She's honest. It's the strangest thing about her.

She stood on the bottom step at bedtime, blue in the lamplight,
and said, "Lend them. Just for a while. Just for the project.
When it's done, nobody will be the wrong color again.
Nobody. Not even me."

And Moon did the thing she does. She looked.
Not at Blooey. At what Blooey was dreaming of wanting.
And there it was: a long table, very long,
and every single chair with somebody blue in it,
and nobody, anywhere, looking twice.

"No," said Moon. Softly. The way you say it to someone you like.
"Then the slow way," said Blooey, and went back down.

*Art:* The bottom step of the great stair at night, where the stair meets the dark sea, lit by one warm gold lantern. Blooey stands on the bottom step, blue in the lamplight, hands folded, calm, asking. Moon hovers a step above her, looking gently at her, and above Blooey's head a soft dream-cloud shows what Moon sees: a very long table with many chairs and small blue figures seated all along it. Baby Lady peeks from behind Moon. Round full moon over the sea. Nothing frightening; a quiet, sad, kind moment.

## 8. The bubble that doesn't pop

The witch had been busy. Witches are.
She'd blown one bubble, only one, for a night and a day,
and it was the size of a hill, and then a mountain,
and it did not pop, because she'd stirred a don't-pop into it,
and it rolled.

It rolled up the sea road, slow as weather.
It rolled over the pool and the rocks and the bottom step.
It went over the castle like a hand over a candle,
and inside it, everything, all at once, went blue.

Blue sea. Blue stair. Blue moon, round and blue.
Cottontail's spots: blue. Winds' clouds: bluer.
Dirt's beans, blue. Pink Shine, blue, and not a bit pink.
Moon's pink hair, blue. Baby Lady's ears, blue.
Everything matched.
And Blooey walked in through the wall of it, smiling,
and held out her hands for the shine.

*Art:* BLUE WASH begins. A single enormous translucent blue bubble, bigger than the castle, has rolled over Castle Everstair and its stair; seen from outside at a distance, the castle, the stair, the sea and the round full moon are all inside it and tinted blue. Along the sea road a small blue figure (Blooey) walks calmly through the bubble's wall toward the castle. Outside the bubble the night is still indigo and lavender; inside, every color is a shade of blue. Elysian tiny in the distance behind, cauldron glowing green. No other people.

## 9. Nothing to catch

And there wasn't any.

Oh, it was there. Moon still had every power she'd ever had.
But bubbles catch gold. That's how the witch made them.
And there wasn't a gold thing left in the world to see.
Blooey had painted it out. All of it. Herself.

She reached for Moon's wings. Blue. Just blue.
She reached for Baby Lady's bark. It came out blue, and got away.
"Where IS it?" said Blooey, and for the first time she sounded like the others.
"Same place it always was," said Moon. "You just can't tell it from the rest."

The witch said a word that means "oh dear" in witch.
"Two," said Blooey, very quietly. "That was two."
But the fort kept growing. It didn't need the shine to grow.
It only needed nobody to stop it.

*Art:* BLUE WASH. Inside the fort, on the castle terrace: everything tinted blue, the stone, the sea, the round moon overhead. Blooey stands with her hands held out in front of Moon and Baby Lady, and small blue bubbles hover around the twins' wings finding nothing to catch, drifting away empty. Moon's hair and wings are blue-tinted, Baby Lady all blue-tinted, but their eyes are calm. Blooey's face shows the first real puzzlement. Elysian in the background with a hand to her head. Beyond, the bubble wall still swelling outward over the woods.

## 10. Everybody tries

Cottontail clawed the wall of the bubble.
It stretched like jelly. It wobbled. It did not pop.
Winds blew, and the whole fort drifted a little to the left,
with everybody in it. "Sorry," said Winds.
Dirt lay down in the blue beans and closed his eyes,
and things grew, but they grew blue, and that wasn't the problem.

And out past the wall the blue went on going:
over the woods, over the wolves (Poodle said a wolf word; Hairs said his),
over the tower, over the tower sisters,
over the one grey place, which had been grey for so long,
and now, at last, wasn't. It was blue instead.
Nobody had asked it.

Moon reached in the purse for something that wasn't real,
and pulled out a ladder, a kite, a maybe,
and every one of them was blue, and none of them helped.

*Art:* BLUE WASH. The castle garden inside the fort, all blues. The young jaguar cub Cottontail rears up clawing at the stretchy bubble wall, which dents inward like jelly around her paws; Winds beside her blowing a gust that ripples the wall; Dirt lying flat in the bean rows with sprouts coming up blue around him. Moon kneels with the purse, holding a blue ladder, a blue kite and a blue glowing maybe-puff, all useless. Baby Lady sits, thinking, apart from the others. Everything blue, round blue moon above. No rabbits, no other animals.

## 11. What Baby Lady heard

Now, everybody hears Baby Lady. That's the rule.
The fish hear her. The hawk hears her.
But here's the other half, that nobody had noticed:
Baby Lady hears everybody.

She'd heard Blooey say "the only one."
She'd heard "I've been counting."
And she trotted (she trots; it's the tail) to the middle of the blue,
and looked up at the blue girl, and said:

"You don't want the world. That's a lot of world.
You want somebody. You want one somebody who's the same."

Blooey didn't say anything.
When you're right about somebody, they usually don't.

*Art:* BLUE WASH. A quiet two-shot in the middle of the blue castle courtyard: Baby Lady, small, looking up, and Blooey looking down at her. Everything blue, softly lit, the round blue moon behind. The purse lies on the ground beside Baby Lady. Nobody else in the picture. Gentle, still, close.

## 12. Blue on purpose

So Baby Lady picked up the purse in her mouth,
and she tapped herself with it,
and she thought, very hard, of blue.

And she went blue. All of her. Ears, wings, tail, the tiny curl on top.
A small blue puppy, sitting down in front of a small blue girl.

"There," said Baby Lady. "Now you match somebody. Me.
You didn't need everybody. You needed one.
One is plenty. One is a whole lot, actually."

And Blooey looked down at the one blue puppy
in the whole blue world,
and it was the only thing in it she could see.

*Art:* BLUE WASH, but composed so the two of them are the focus: Baby Lady has just tapped herself with the purse (held in her mouth) and is now truly, fully blue, the same matte soft blue as Blooey, sitting up straight in front of her. Blooey has gone down on her knees to be level with her, one hand just reaching out. Around them the blue world fades soft and out of focus; the only sharp things are one blue puppy and one blue girl. Round blue moon above.

## 13. A fort is for keeping out

Here's a thing about forts:
they're for keeping something out.
And Blooey, standing there, couldn't think what.

The bubble felt it first. Bubbles know.
It wobbled, once, and let go,
and the whole enormous don't-pop fort
popped, the quiet way, like a held breath let out,
and there was the sky again, and the sea, and the stair,
all of it still blue, all of it soaking wet.

"Ridiculous," said Elysian, and stirred her cauldron the other way,
and picked it up, and went back under the bottom step,
where she stayed exactly as evil as before.
Some steps stay wobbly. That's just steps.

*Art:* The enormous bubble is popping: a wide shot of Castle Everstair as the fort's wall bursts softly into a shower of fine blue droplets and mist, the indigo night sky reappearing above through the gap, everything below still tinted blue and glistening wet. In the foreground, Elysian, sour-faced, carries her green-glowing cauldron under one arm and ducks down beneath the bottom step of the stair where the sea begins. Blooey and Baby Lady (blue) small in the middle distance, watching the sky come back. Round full moon returning to its true pale silver at the top.

## 14. The colors come home

Then the twins went round with the purse,
the way you'd go round with a candle,
tapping things back.

Tap: the sea went blue the proper way, which is different.
Tap: the stair went gold. Tap: Moon's hair went pink.
Tap, tap, tap: three sets of jaguar spots.
Pink Shine got his pink back and shook it like a wet dog, which he is.

And when they got to the end of everything,
to the one grey place, far out where the dark begins,
they tapped it, too. And it didn't go grey.
It went green, and gold, and sunset, and sea,
every color it had been missing, all at once,
like it had been holding its breath since Book Seven.

And something out there hummed one note,
and it was nearly, nearly right.

*Art:* Full color returning. Moon flies along the stair tapping steps back to gold with the purse, color spreading out behind each tap like paint in water; Baby Lady (still blue) taps Cottontail's spots back to black and gold; Pink Shine mid-shake, spraying pink; Winds and Dirt getting their colors back. In the far distance at the top corner, the one grey place is blooming into green, gold and sunset colors for the first time. Round full moon, pale silver. No rabbits, no other animals.

## 15. One is plenty

Blooey stayed blue. Of course she did.
Nobody offered to tap her, and she'd have said no.
Blue's hers. She likes it. That was never the trouble.

Baby Lady's blue faded by morning, mostly.
One ear took until Thursday.
She didn't mind. She'd have kept it longer, if asked.

And in the garden, next to Blooey's seed-spot,
a fort went up that night. A small one. A pillow one.
Two blankets, one chair, a lantern,
and a sign, in Baby Lady's writing (which is mostly paw),
that said ROOM FOR ONE MORE.

Blooey sat inside it and counted.
"One," she said. And then, because the puppy came in: "Two."
And then she stopped counting, which she had never done.

*Art:* The castle garden at night in full warm color, gold flowers (the blossoms from the old pot shards) glowing in the bean rows. A small cozy pillow fort made of two blankets draped over one chair, lit from inside by a gold lantern, with a small blank wooden sign propped at its door (no letters, no writing visible). Blooey sits inside, blue and content; Baby Lady, with one ear still faintly blue, is crawling in to join her. Round full moon over the castle. Nobody else.

## 16. Bowls

The soup that night had a lot of bowls.
Two for two sisters, one crown, one curl.
A mama's bowl, a papa's. Three jaguar bowls, all spots.
Two wolf bowls (one enormous). Five sister bowls in a row,
and the blue one wasn't at the end this time. She was in the middle.
Somebody had moved her chair.

And one more bowl, full, set, at the end,
for anyone stormy who might not be there.
There's always one. There always will be.

Then the purse went back on the pillow, where it lives now,
with a maybe inside it, squeaking a little,
and the round yellow moon came up over the stair (yellow, not blue),
and everyone matched exactly as much as they wanted to,
which was: not very. Which was: enough.

Goodnight.

*Art:* The long soup table on the castle terrace at night, full warm color, gold lanterns, round full yellow moon over the stair. Seated: Moon and Baby Lady side by side, Purple Shine and Pink Shine, the three young jaguar cubs Cottontail, Winds and Dirt, Poodle and enormous Hairs at the far side, and the sisters Evilest, Cheeblest, Beedlist in a row with Blooey seated in the middle of them rather than the end. Shock stands far off at the horizon edge of the sea, masked, small. At the end of the table one full untouched soup bowl before an empty chair. Every character exactly once, no other people, no other animals.

**The End — of the eighth one.**

## Wonder Together

1. Blooey wanted the whole world to be blue so that everybody would match her. Baby Lady figured out she really only wanted one friend who was the same. Have you ever wanted everyone to like what you like? What did you really want underneath?
2. The purse can pull out things that aren't real: a ladder to a cloud, a kite the color of Tuesday, a maybe. If you reached into the purse, what would you pull out?
3. Baby Lady turned herself blue on purpose so Blooey wouldn't be the only one. That was a kind thing to do. Can you think of a time you did something so somebody else wouldn't feel alone?
4. At the end, everybody at the table was a different color, and that was enough. Talk about it together: what makes you feel like you belong somewhere, even when you're not the same as everyone else?

**Look and find:** The purse is in almost every picture, and its clasp stays a tiny bit gold even when the whole world goes blue. Can you find it? And watch for the one grey place far off in the corner: it's grey at the start, blue in the middle, and full of color by the end.
