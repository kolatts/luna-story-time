A wide, quiet, colorless grey meadow far from the castle at night: grey grass, a row of grey stones, a soft grey sky, the lavender castle only a distant warm-gold speck on the horizon. In the middle of all the grey stands the only color for miles: a small all-blue girl, calm and patient, and at her feet one stone has just turned bright blue. Beside her, the very old hunched witch stirs a round black cauldron glowing sick green and blows large slow translucent blue bubbles through a bent black straw; a few blue bubbles drift up into the grey sky. Her long black hair, lit from behind by the cauldron glow, falls into the faint shape of a skull made only of hair strands. A perfectly round full moon, pale grey, in the sky (never a crescent). No other people, no animals. Blooey: a girl the same size as the other small sisters; every bit of her skin is a smooth soft matte blue — face, ears, hands, all of it, like a girl painted gently blue — strange and calm but never frightening, never gory; long straight deep-blue hair; plain long midnight-blue high-collared dress; a quiet patient face with a small unreadable smile. Elysian: very old witch, one hundred and five years old, small and hunched like a hook; deeply wrinkled pale grey-lavender face with a long nose and heavy-lidded eyes; floor-length black hair that, when light shines through it, drapes into the unmistakable shape of a skull — two round sockets, a jaw, a grin — made only of falling hair strands, never a real skull, and it shifts when she moves; the skull shape appears ONLY in her hair, never on her clothing; plain ragged high-collared black robe with no pattern, no markings, and no skull on it; carries a thin black wand; her cave holds a round black cauldron glowing sick green, a worn twig broom, and an enormous open spell book; no pet and no helper; ugly-scary but calm and certain, never gleeful, never gory or horrific.
