Book cover composition: in a misty blue-green wood, an enormous gentle grey wolf with a glossy black tail sits and leans his huge head all the way down toward a tiny two-year-old girl who stands looking straight up at him, her small hand raised; his impossibly long silver hair spills out around them and away into the trees, catching the light like rope and river; a seven-year-old girl in a moss-green cloak and crooked tin crown stands just behind her little sister with one hand out. The wolf's mouth is closed, his amber eyes enormous and kind — no teeth, nothing frightening. Warm gold light breaking through the trees, floating sparkles. Open sky and mist in the upper third for the title. No other people, no other animals. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening. Princess Fern: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, dark curly hair in two round puffs, a small crooked tin crown sitting slightly askew; moss-green hooded cloak over a mustard-yellow dress, sturdy brown boots, a small woven berry basket; big dark eyes, a determined chin. Baby Bee: a two-year-old toddler girl, warm brown skin, one wispy curl on top of her head, very round cheeks, a honey-yellow knitted romper with tiny round buttons, bare feet; always holding a soft grey knitted blanket; calm, unbothered, enormous dark eyes.
