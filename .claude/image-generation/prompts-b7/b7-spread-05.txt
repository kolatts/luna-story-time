Inside the deep blue-green Bramblewood, dim and dappled with shafts of warm light: long silvery strands of hair are strung everywhere like harp strings and cobwebby ropes, hooked on thorns, looped through branches, drifting over roots — the whole wood laced with silver. Princess Fern is tangled mid-step, her cloak and crown snagged on silver strands, eyes wide, turning toward a dark gap between two trunks; Baby Bee holds a fistful of her sister's cloak, unafraid. Dramatic but gentle, no monster visible, nothing frightening. No other children and no other people in the picture; each character appears exactly once. No other animals. Princess Fern: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, dark curly hair in two round puffs, a small crooked tin crown sitting slightly askew; moss-green hooded cloak over a mustard-yellow dress, sturdy brown boots, a small woven berry basket; big dark eyes, a determined chin. Baby Bee: a two-year-old toddler girl, warm brown skin, one wispy curl on top of her head, very round cheeks, a honey-yellow knitted romper with tiny round buttons, bare feet; always holding a soft grey knitted blanket; calm, unbothered, enormous dark eyes.
