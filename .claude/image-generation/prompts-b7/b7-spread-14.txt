Warm cozy supper inside the big hollow-tree den: a low stone table with three wooden bowls, a covered pot of orange soup steaming, a loaf of brown bread and a heap of blackberries; Baby Bee eating happily with both hands, face a mess; Princess Fern mid-second-helping with her crown pushed back; the enormous grey wolf curled around the whole table, chin resting on the stone, just watching them with shining amber eyes, mouth closed, deeply happy. Firelight gold against deep indigo evening outside the round doorway. No other children and no other people in the picture; each character appears exactly once. No other animals. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening. Princess Fern: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, dark curly hair in two round puffs, a small crooked tin crown sitting slightly askew; moss-green hooded cloak over a mustard-yellow dress, sturdy brown boots, a small woven berry basket; big dark eyes, a determined chin. Baby Bee: a two-year-old toddler girl, warm brown skin, one wispy curl on top of her head, very round cheeks, a honey-yellow knitted romper with tiny round buttons, bare feet; always holding a soft grey knitted blanket; calm, unbothered, enormous dark eyes.
