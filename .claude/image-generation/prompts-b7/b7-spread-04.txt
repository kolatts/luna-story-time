At the tall arched bramble-and-thorn gate on the hilltop, late afternoon wind blowing: the soft grey knitted blanket has landed draped high on the thorny arch, out of reach, one corner fluttering. Princess Fern stands with hands on hips glaring up at it, cloak whipping; Baby Bee stands beside her pointing up at the blanket, perfectly calm. Beyond the gate the misty blue-green wood waits. No other children and no other people in the picture; each character appears exactly once. No other animals. Princess Fern: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, dark curly hair in two round puffs, a small crooked tin crown sitting slightly askew; moss-green hooded cloak over a mustard-yellow dress, sturdy brown boots, a small woven berry basket; big dark eyes, a determined chin. Baby Bee: a two-year-old toddler girl, warm brown skin, one wispy curl on top of her head, very round cheeks, a honey-yellow knitted romper with tiny round buttons, bare feet; always holding a soft grey knitted blanket; calm, unbothered, enormous dark eyes.
