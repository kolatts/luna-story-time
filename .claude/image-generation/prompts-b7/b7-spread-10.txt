The enormous grey wolf has sat down heavily in a great soft pile of his own silver hair, shoulders low, looking almost shy, head tilted, huge amber eyes on the children, mouth gently open mid-word, no teeth. Princess Fern has stepped closer and is listening hard, head tipped; Baby Bee has already climbed into the nearest coil of silver hair and is sitting in it like a nest. Warm intimate light in the blue-green clearing, sparkles. No other children and no other people in the picture; each character appears exactly once. No other animals. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening. Princess Fern: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, dark curly hair in two round puffs, a small crooked tin crown sitting slightly askew; moss-green hooded cloak over a mustard-yellow dress, sturdy brown boots, a small woven berry basket; big dark eyes, a determined chin. Baby Bee: a two-year-old toddler girl, warm brown skin, one wispy curl on top of her head, very round cheeks, a honey-yellow knitted romper with tiny round buttons, bare feet; always holding a soft grey knitted blanket; calm, unbothered, enormous dark eyes.
