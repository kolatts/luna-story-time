Low storybook view from behind and below: tiny Baby Bee stands alone in the open, bare feet in the moss, grey blanket tucked under one arm, head tipped all the way back to look up at the enormous grey wolf who towers over her, leaning down toward her with astonished gentle amber eyes and closed mouth. Princess Fern is a few steps behind, frozen, hand half-raised. The whole clearing glows — sparkles and floating pollen hanging still in the hush. Warm gold light breaking through blue-green trees. No other children and no other people in the picture; each character appears exactly once. No other animals. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening. Princess Fern: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, dark curly hair in two round puffs, a small crooked tin crown sitting slightly askew; moss-green hooded cloak over a mustard-yellow dress, sturdy brown boots, a small woven berry basket; big dark eyes, a determined chin. Baby Bee: a two-year-old toddler girl, warm brown skin, one wispy curl on top of her head, very round cheeks, a honey-yellow knitted romper with tiny round buttons, bare feet; always holding a soft grey knitted blanket; calm, unbothered, enormous dark eyes.
