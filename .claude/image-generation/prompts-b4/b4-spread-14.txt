At the boundary between silver rain-light and old black dark: Moon, small and gentle, holds one open hand out into the falling rain toward the ancient witch, who stands just beyond the rain's reach with her broom and wand and great book gathered in her arms, half turned away toward the deep of the cave; the skull-shape in her long hair holding perfectly still; behind her the cave floor slopes down into layer after layer of deeper dark. Rain-silver on one side, black on the other, one thin bright line between them. Dignified, sad, quiet; never gory. Princess Moon: young girl, long wavy pink hair, large dark-blue eyes, rosy cheeks; delicate translucent golden fairy wings with fine filigree veining; silver iridescent mermaid tail with pearl-sheen scales; small gold crown topped with a crescent moon; pearl necklace with a gold crescent pendant. Elysian: very old witch, one hundred and five years old, small and hunched like a hook; deeply wrinkled pale grey-lavender face with a long nose and heavy-lidded eyes; floor-length black hair that, when light shines through it, drapes into the unmistakable shape of a skull — two round sockets, a jaw, a grin — made only of falling hair, never a real skull, and it shifts when she moves; plain ragged high-collared black robe; carries a thin black wand; her cave holds a round black cauldron glowing sick green, a worn twig broom, and an enormous open spell book; no pet and no helper; ugly-scary but calm and certain, never gleeful, never gory or horrific.
