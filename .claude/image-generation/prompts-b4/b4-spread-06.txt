Deep beneath the castle: a vast cave hall of wet black stone lit by the strong sick-green glow of a bubbling cauldron that keeps the whole scene clearly visible — beside it a worn twig broom standing against the wall, a thin black wand, and an enormous open spell book on a stone ledge; at the cauldron the ancient hunched witch stirs slowly, her floor-length black hair catching the green light so it drapes into the clear shape of a skull — two sockets, a jaw, a grin made only of hair; at the far cave mouth, the children tiny and huddled in the glowing stair doorway. Bone-white highlights on wet stone; unsettling but gentle, never gory. Elysian: very old witch, one hundred and five years old, small and hunched like a hook; deeply wrinkled pale grey-lavender face with a long nose and heavy-lidded eyes; floor-length black hair that, when light shines through it, drapes into the unmistakable shape of a skull — two round sockets, a jaw, a grin — made only of falling hair, never a real skull, and it shifts when she moves; plain ragged high-collared black robe; carries a thin black wand; her cave holds a round black cauldron glowing sick green, a worn twig broom, and an enormous open spell book; no pet and no helper; ugly-scary but calm and certain, never gleeful, never gory or horrific.
