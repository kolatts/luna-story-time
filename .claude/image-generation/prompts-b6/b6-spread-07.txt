Night in the twins' bedroom, lit warm by one small bedside lamp: Moon and the small winged puppy back to back at opposite ends of the same big bed, both glaring tiredly over their shoulders at each other, the blanket tugged in both directions, the window curtains seeming to lean apart in shock; low in one dark corner of the picture, glimpsed through a glowing floor-crack, the small clay pot now a quarter full of swirling gold light, cradled in two blue hands. Gentle comedy in the quarrel, never mean; deep indigo night outside the window with a perfectly round full moon (never a crescent). Princess Moon: young girl, long wavy pink hair, large dark-blue eyes, rosy cheeks; delicate translucent golden fairy wings with fine filigree veining; silver iridescent mermaid tail with pearl-sheen scales; small gold crown topped with a crescent moon; pearl necklace with a gold crescent pendant. Baby Lady: small fluffy cocker-spaniel puppy, golden-cream curly ears, dark round eyes; iridescent pearl-white fairy wings, smaller and softer than Moon's; pale silver-pink mermaid tail in place of back legs — she has no back legs and no back paws, her puppy body ends in the mermaid tail, front paws only; tiny gold crescent charm on a pearl collar. Leeblebeest: a girl the same size as the other small sisters; every bit of her skin is a smooth soft matte blue — face, ears, hands, all of it, like a girl painted gently blue — strange and calm but never frightening, never gory; long straight deep-blue hair; plain long midnight-blue high-collared dress; a quiet patient face with a small unreadable smile; she carries a little round clay pot of plain unglazed brown clay, small as a teacup, cupped carefully in both hands.
