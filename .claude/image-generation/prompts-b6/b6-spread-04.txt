Deep beneath the castle in a stone cellar lit by one warm amber lantern: an all-blue girl in a midnight-blue dress sits calmly on an old wooden crate, holding out a small empty clay pot in both hands, her blue face gentle and strange but never frightening; facing her in the lantern glow — Evilest staring with her arms coming uncrossed, Beedlist clutching her small black book to her chest, and the tall masked sister in her grey cloak standing very still at the edge of the light. Every sister is a small child except the tall masked one. Deep indigo shadows, one clear warm light source. Never gory, never horrific. Leeblebeest: a girl the same size as the other small sisters; every bit of her skin is a smooth soft matte blue — face, ears, hands, all of it, like a girl painted gently blue — strange and calm but never frightening, never gory; long straight deep-blue hair; plain long midnight-blue high-collared dress; a quiet patient face with a small unreadable smile; she carries a little round clay pot of plain unglazed brown clay, small as a teacup, cupped carefully in both hands. Evilest: pale girl, black hair, tall spiky black crown, high-collared black gown, arms crossed, sharp confident smile. Beedlist: pale girl dressed entirely in black: long straight black hair, plain long black high-collared dress, black shoes, black ribbon; no crown; no color anywhere on her; small calm unreadable face; perfectly still posture, hands folded in front of her. Shock: a tall slender girl, a head taller than the other sisters, always standing very straight; long storm-grey hair falling past her waist; a long charcoal-grey hooded cloak whose hem fades into soft mist; grey gloves; her whole face hidden behind a smooth dark carved mask shaped like a gently scowling storm-cloud creature with two small carved fangs at its frown, round dark eye-holes, a thin hairline crack down one cheek, and a small simple tipped-sideways crescent moon — one clean thin C-shaped curve — scratched above the brow; the mask never comes off and no part of her face is ever visible; unsettling but gentle, never gory or horrific.
