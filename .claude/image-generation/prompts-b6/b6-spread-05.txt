In the lantern-lit stone cellar, the all-blue girl stands explaining her plan, slowly turning the small empty clay pot in her blue hands, wearing a small patient smile; around the single warm amber lantern the other three lean in — Evilest grinning despite herself, Beedlist watching over the top of her black book, the tall masked sister motionless in her grey cloak; on the stone wall behind them, soft indigo shadow-shapes of a creaking door, a falling drip, and a small moth. Every sister is a small child except the tall masked one. One clear warm light source against deep indigo. Never gory, never horrific. Leeblebeest: a girl the same size as the other small sisters; every bit of her skin is a smooth soft matte blue — face, ears, hands, all of it, like a girl painted gently blue — strange and calm but never frightening, never gory; long straight deep-blue hair; plain long midnight-blue high-collared dress; a quiet patient face with a small unreadable smile; she carries a little round clay pot of plain unglazed brown clay, small as a teacup, cupped carefully in both hands. Evilest: pale girl, black hair, tall spiky black crown, high-collared black gown, arms crossed, sharp confident smile. Beedlist: pale girl dressed entirely in black: long straight black hair, plain long black high-collared dress, black shoes, black ribbon; no crown; no color anywhere on her; small calm unreadable face; perfectly still posture, hands folded in front of her. Shock: a tall slender girl, a head taller than the other sisters, always standing very straight; long storm-grey hair falling past her waist; a long charcoal-grey hooded cloak whose hem fades into soft mist; grey gloves; her whole face hidden behind a smooth dark carved mask shaped like a gently scowling storm-cloud creature with two small carved fangs at its frown, round dark eye-holes, a thin hairline crack down one cheek, and a small simple tipped-sideways crescent moon — one clean thin C-shaped curve — scratched above the brow; the mask never comes off and no part of her face is ever visible; unsettling but gentle, never gory or horrific.
