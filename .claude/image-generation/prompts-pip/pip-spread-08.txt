Night at the low stone wall on the edge of the hilltop, fog everywhere below: Bo standing up on the wall pointing out into the fog, counting, his face very serious; Nell beside him with a hand on his back, looking where he points; the huge plum-purple dragon's head resting behind them both. Far below, a scattering of tiny warm village lights half-drowned in fog, and one dark gap where a light should be; the thin thread of a mountain road disappearing into white. Deep indigo and lantern gold, hushed and worried but gentle. No other people, no other children, no other animals. Bo: a four-year-old boy, Nell's little brother — the same warm brown skin and dark curls, round cheeks, huge serious dark eyes; a bright red knitted woolen hat with a floppy pom-pom that he never takes off; a mustard-yellow scarf wound twice around his neck; he carries one stick of kindling like it is an important job. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Plum: a very large grown dragon, three or four times Pip's size — deep plum-purple all over with translucent lavender-violet wing membranes, a long neck and a long tail with a soft violet fin running along it, four gently curving lilac horns, calm dark-violet eyes under heavy kind lids, big rounded feet with blunt pale claws, a broad flat nose that puffs small silver smoke rings; unhurried and gentle, like a hillside that decided to be a dragon.
