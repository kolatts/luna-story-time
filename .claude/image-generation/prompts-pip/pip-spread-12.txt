Night on the hilltop: the huge plum-purple dragon lying down full-length along the low stone wall like a long purple hill, one enormous lavender wing lifted and arched over Nell and Bo, who are tucked safely behind the wall underneath it as if under a roof, Nell calling out from beneath the wing; far out on the open hilltop, well away from them, the small rose-pink dragon stands alone facing the tall iron beacon, wings beginning to spread, chest filling with air. Both children clearly far back and sheltered. Deep indigo, fog, faint rose light. No other people, no other children, no other animals. Plum: a very large grown dragon, three or four times Pip's size — deep plum-purple all over with translucent lavender-violet wing membranes, a long neck and a long tail with a soft violet fin running along it, four gently curving lilac horns, calm dark-violet eyes under heavy kind lids, big rounded feet with blunt pale claws, a broad flat nose that puffs small silver smoke rings; unhurried and gentle, like a hillside that decided to be a dragon. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Bo: a four-year-old boy, Nell's little brother — the same warm brown skin and dark curls, round cheeks, huge serious dark eyes; a bright red knitted woolen hat with a floppy pom-pom that he never takes off; a mustard-yellow scarf wound twice around his neck; he carries one stick of kindling like it is an important job. Pip: a small young dragon, about the size of a big dog — warm rose-pink all over, smooth pink scales with a paler blush-pink belly, soft pearly-pink wings with a faint shimmer, a short rounded snout, two tiny blunt lilac horns, big round amber-gold eyes with long lashes, a stubby tail with a small pink fin at the tip; blunt little teeth that never show; one faintly singed patch along a wing edge; clearly a young dragon, never a person, never a child.
