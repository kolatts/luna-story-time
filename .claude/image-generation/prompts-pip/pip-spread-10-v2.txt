Night on the hilltop in fog: Nell turned all the way around, standing perfectly still, looking straight across the wet grass at the small rose-pink dragon, who sits at the far dark edge of the hill with her wings folded tiny, lit faintly pink; fog lying between them; behind Nell, the dark shape of the old iron beacon: an open EMPTY rusty fire-BASKET of iron bars on top of a plain tall wooden pole, completely dark and unlit, holding no fire, no lamp and no glass — it is NOT a lighthouse and NOT a lantern tower, just an empty metal basket on a pole and the huge purple dragon in the dark. A quiet, held-breath moment in deep indigo and soft rose. No other people, no other children, no other animals. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Pip: a small young dragon, about the size of a big dog — warm rose-pink all over, smooth pink scales with a paler blush-pink belly, soft pearly-pink wings with a faint shimmer, a short rounded snout, two tiny blunt lilac horns, big round amber-gold eyes with long lashes, a stubby tail with a small pink fin at the tip; blunt little teeth that never show; one faintly singed patch along a wing edge; clearly a young dragon, never a person, never a child. Plum: a very large grown dragon, three or four times Pip's size — deep plum-purple all over with translucent lavender-violet wing membranes, a long neck and a long tail with a soft violet fin running along it, four gently curving lilac horns, calm dark-violet eyes under heavy kind lids, big rounded feet with blunt pale claws, a broad flat nose that puffs small silver smoke rings; unhurried and gentle, like a hillside that decided to be a dragon.
