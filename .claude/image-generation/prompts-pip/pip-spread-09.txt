Night on the foggy hilltop: the huge plum-purple dragon stretched up tall with his long neck extended, breathing a thin gold thread of fire up into a big rusty iron beacon basket on its tall pole, the wood inside black and soaking wet; only steam and a curl of grey smoke coming off it, no flame catching; Nell and Bo watch from behind the low stone wall, well back, faces anxious; the small rose-pink dragon is a soft shape in the fog behind them. Deep indigo night, cold wet fog, a single small gold light source. No other people, no other children, no other animals. Plum: a very large grown dragon, three or four times Pip's size — deep plum-purple all over with translucent lavender-violet wing membranes, a long neck and a long tail with a soft violet fin running along it, four gently curving lilac horns, calm dark-violet eyes under heavy kind lids, big rounded feet with blunt pale claws, a broad flat nose that puffs small silver smoke rings; unhurried and gentle, like a hillside that decided to be a dragon. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Bo: a four-year-old boy, Nell's little brother — the same warm brown skin and dark curls, round cheeks, huge serious dark eyes; a bright red knitted woolen hat with a floppy pom-pom that he never takes off; a mustard-yellow scarf wound twice around his neck; he carries one stick of kindling like it is an important job. Pip: a small young dragon, about the size of a big dog — warm rose-pink all over, smooth pink scales with a paler blush-pink belly, soft pearly-pink wings with a faint shimmer, a short rounded snout, two tiny blunt lilac horns, big round amber-gold eyes with long lashes, a stubby tail with a small pink fin at the tip; blunt little teeth that never show; one faintly singed patch along a wing edge; clearly a young dragon, never a person, never a child.
