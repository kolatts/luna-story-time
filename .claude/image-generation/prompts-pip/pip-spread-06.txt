A grey overcast day in the village lane below the hill: the small rose-pink dragon crouched right down making herself as tiny as she possibly can, wings pressed flat, mouth clamped shut, eyes lowered; standing at a distance around her, four or five grown-up villagers in shawls and long coats with their arms folded, talking about her — wary but not cruel, none of them close to her; stone cottage walls behind, and above the rooftops the hill with its iron beacon pole. Muted lavender-grey palette, one small warm ember-glow escaping between the little dragon's closed lips. Sad and gentle, nobody shouting. Grown-up villagers only — no children except the dragon is not a child, no other animals. Pip: a small young dragon, about the size of a big dog — warm rose-pink all over, smooth pink scales with a paler blush-pink belly, soft pearly-pink wings with a faint shimmer, a short rounded snout, two tiny blunt lilac horns, big round amber-gold eyes with long lashes, a stubby tail with a small pink fin at the tip; blunt little teeth that never show; one faintly singed patch along a wing edge; clearly a young dragon, never a person, never a child.
