Wide establishing shot in late-afternoon gold: a single tall green hill at the end of a winding dirt road, the road petering out into grass; dark blue mountains standing behind it; on the hilltop Nell and Bo stack a neat woodpile together beside a small stone cottage and a low stone wall, with an old rusty empty iron beacon basket on a tall pole at the edge of the hilltop; far below, a small village of warm lit windows in the valley. Soft pink clouds just beginning along the horizon. Cozy, storybook scale. No other people, no other children, no animals. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Bo: a four-year-old boy, Nell's little brother — the same warm brown skin and dark curls, round cheeks, huge serious dark eyes; a bright red knitted woolen hat with a floppy pom-pom that he never takes off; a mustard-yellow scarf wound twice around his neck; he carries one stick of kindling like it is an important job.
