A remembered scene in soft spring daylight, painted a little hazier at the edges like a memory: a great oak tree beside a hilltop cottage with one side of it blackened and gently smoking, an old rope swing burnt away to a stub, drifting smoke and orange sparks floating up into a pale sky; below it the small rose-pink dragon stands with both wings clapped over her own mouth in horror, eyes huge; Nell and Bo stand well back, unhurt, mouths open. Dramatic but soft and safe — no flames near anyone, nobody hurt, no building on fire, the cottage completely whole and untouched with no smoke and no marks on its roof, nothing frightening. No other people, no other children, no other animals. Pip: a small young dragon, about the size of a big dog — warm rose-pink all over, smooth pink scales with a paler blush-pink belly, soft pearly-pink wings with a faint shimmer, a short rounded snout, two tiny blunt lilac horns, big round amber-gold eyes with long lashes, a stubby tail with a small pink fin at the tip; blunt little teeth that never show; one faintly singed patch along a wing edge; clearly a young dragon, never a person, never a child. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Bo: a four-year-old boy, Nell's little brother — the same warm brown skin and dark curls, round cheeks, huge serious dark eyes; a bright red knitted woolen hat with a floppy pom-pom that he never takes off; a mustard-yellow scarf wound twice around his neck; he carries one stick of kindling like it is an important job.
