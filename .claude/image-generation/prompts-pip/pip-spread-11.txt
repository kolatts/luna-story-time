Night and fog, close and tender: the small rose-pink dragon with her head held low, eyes enormous and shining, shrinking back a little; Nell standing right in front of her, calm, one hand held out; beside them Bo has taken off his red woolen hat — his dark curls showing, bare-headed for the only time in the whole book — and is setting the hat carefully on top of the low stone wall, very solemn about it. Faint warm glow from campfire embers off to one side. Deep indigo, rose and gold. Gentle: no fear in the children's faces. No other people, no other children, no other animals. Pip: a small young dragon, about the size of a big dog — warm rose-pink all over, smooth pink scales with a paler blush-pink belly, soft pearly-pink wings with a faint shimmer, a short rounded snout, two tiny blunt lilac horns, big round amber-gold eyes with long lashes, a stubby tail with a small pink fin at the tip; blunt little teeth that never show; one faintly singed patch along a wing edge; clearly a young dragon, never a person, never a child. Nell: a seven-year-old girl, warm brown skin, thick dark curls tied back with a strip of yellow cloth, large dark eyes, a gap in her front teeth when she grins; a patched grey-green wool coat too big at the sleeves, muddy boots; a small tin whistle on a cord around her neck. Bo: a four-year-old boy, Nell's little brother — the same warm brown skin and dark curls, round cheeks, huge serious dark eyes; a bright red knitted woolen hat with a floppy pom-pom that he never takes off; a mustard-yellow scarf wound twice around his neck; he carries one stick of kindling like it is an important job.
