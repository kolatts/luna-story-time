A wide sad grey landscape: bare grey berry bushes, grey grass, a grey stream, a grey sky — a whole world with its color rinsed out, painted in soft watercolor greys and washed lavender-greys, beautiful rather than gloomy. Crossing it, the small lean curly grey wolf trots ahead nose-down and sneaky, and behind him the enormous grey wolf plods with his nose deep in an empty grey berry bush, his endless silver mane dragging a wide soft trail through the grass behind him. A single warm gold lantern hangs forgotten on a fence post — the only color in the picture. Perfectly round full moon (never a crescent) low in the sky. Wolves only — no dogs, no poodles, no other animals. Poodle: a small lean young grey WOLF (a wolf, never a dog, never a poodle) — barely a quarter the size of Hairs; a narrow clever face, tall pointed ears with one ear notched, bright amber eyes, a long bushy tail with a white tip; scruffy charcoal-and-silver fur that curls oddly tight all over him, which is how he got his name; a small tattered moss-green scarf knotted at his neck; stands and walks on four paws; a sideways scheming grin with small blunt friendly teeth; talkative, mischievous, never fierce, never frightening. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening.
