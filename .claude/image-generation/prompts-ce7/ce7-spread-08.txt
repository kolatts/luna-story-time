Wild funny motion: the enormous grey wolf gallops away from the castle into the grey woods with the entire birthday feast bundled up in his own vast silver mane — cakes, berry towers, sugar moons and star-shaped cheese riding along in the hair and tumbling out behind him in a comic trail — while the small curly wolf in the green scarf races alongside, laughing, tongue out, one whole cake balanced on his head. Behind them the golden castle terrace glows, its long table suddenly bare. Colorless grey forest, warm gold light spilling from the castle behind. Joyful and comic, never frightening, no bared teeth. Wolves only — no dogs, no poodles, no other animals. Poodle: a small lean young grey WOLF (a wolf, never a dog, never a poodle) — barely a quarter the size of Hairs; a narrow clever face, tall pointed ears with one ear notched, bright amber eyes, a long bushy tail with a white tip; scruffy charcoal-and-silver fur that curls oddly tight all over him, which is how he got his name; a small tattered moss-green scarf knotted at his neck; stands and walks on four paws; a sideways scheming grin with small blunt friendly teeth; talkative, mischievous, never fierce, never frightening. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening.
