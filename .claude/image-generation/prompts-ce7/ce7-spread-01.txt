Deep in a colorless grey forest at dusk: a small lean curly-furred grey wolf in a tattered green scarf stands on a mossy log drawing a plan in the dirt with one claw, gesturing grandly — and looming behind him, taking up most of the picture, an ENORMOUS gentle grey wolf with huge soft amber eyes, sitting patiently, his hundred-year silver mane pouring off him and trailing away between the trees into the distance. The size difference is comic and enormous. Every tree, leaf and fern is washed to soft grey, as if the color has been rinsed out of the world; only the warm gold of moonlight through the branches and the round full moon above stay true. Cozy, funny, storybook-mischievous — never scary, no bared teeth. Wolves only — no dogs, no poodles, no rabbits, no other animals. Poodle: a small lean young grey WOLF (a wolf, never a dog, never a poodle) — barely a quarter the size of Hairs; a narrow clever face, tall pointed ears with one ear notched, bright amber eyes, a long bushy tail with a white tip; scruffy charcoal-and-silver fur that curls oddly tight all over him, which is how he got his name; a small tattered moss-green scarf knotted at his neck; stands and walks on four paws; a sideways scheming grin with small blunt friendly teeth; talkative, mischievous, never fierce, never frightening. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening.
