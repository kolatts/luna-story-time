Inside a warm crooked little cottage at night, everything leaning slightly sideways: a small round grandmotherly witch in a floppy patched blue pointed hat stirs a big bubbling copper pot on a stove, her round spectacles fogged, laugh lines everywhere. On the shelf beside her stands a fat round glass bottle stoppered with wood, holding a slow swirl of rainbow light — the only real color in the whole picture, throwing colored spots across her face and the rafters. Through the window behind her, the grey colorless world. Herbs, jars and teacups clutter every shelf. Warm gold firelight, deep indigo window. Cozy and safe, absolutely not a scary witch. No other people, no other animals. Witchy: a small round elderly GOOD witch with warm brown skin, deep laugh-line crinkles and a wide kind smile, silver-white hair escaping from under a soft floppy pointed hat of patched moonlit-blue cloth; a patched blue-and-lavender travelling cloak over a simple grey dress, round spectacles, sturdy scuffed boots; she carries a fat round glass potion bottle with a wooden stopper, filled part-way with slow swirling rainbow light; warm and grandmotherly, never sinister, never green-skinned, no warts, not a scary witch.
