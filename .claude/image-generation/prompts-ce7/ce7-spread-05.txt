Night on a grey hillside above the sea road: the small lean curly grey wolf and the enormous silver-maned grey wolf sit side by side with both noses tipped straight up and eyes closed in bliss, breathing in a thin drifting golden ribbon of delicious smell that curls up from the distant castle far below — the smell drawn as a glowing gold sparkle-trail winding through the colorless grey landscape, the only warmth in the picture. The small wolf's green scarf flutters; the enormous one is drooling just slightly, his silver mane spilling down the whole hillside behind them. Perfectly round full moon (never a crescent) behind them. Funny and adorable, never menacing. Wolves only — no dogs, no poodles, no other animals. Poodle: a small lean young grey WOLF (a wolf, never a dog, never a poodle) — barely a quarter the size of Hairs; a narrow clever face, tall pointed ears with one ear notched, bright amber eyes, a long bushy tail with a white tip; scruffy charcoal-and-silver fur that curls oddly tight all over him, which is how he got his name; a small tattered moss-green scarf knotted at his neck; stands and walks on four paws; a sideways scheming grin with small blunt friendly teeth; talkative, mischievous, never fierce, never frightening. Hairs: an enormous gentle grey wolf, soft dove-grey fur, glossy jet-black tail; extraordinarily long silver-grey hair growing from his head, neck and shoulders and flowing far behind him in ropes and drifts, a hundred years long and never brushed; huge soft amber eyes under kind heavy brows, rounded ears, big dark nose; mouth always closed or softly open — never bared teeth, never snarling, never fierce, never a predator; shy and slow and mountain-sized, like a hill that stood up. Gentle and sad-eyed, not frightening.
